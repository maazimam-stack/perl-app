Community Service and Service-Learning Policy

Affected Department(s) or Parties: Carle Illinois College of Medicine Students
Effective Date: July 1, 2024
Revision Date: n/a
Next Review Date: July 1, 2017
Policy Number: SL-5
Purpose: This policy addresses LCME element 6.6. Additionally, the policy provides greater
clarity regarding service learning versus community service. The policy also outlines the criteria
for special recognition for service learning.
Scope: This policy is for Carle Illinois College of Medicine students (M1-M4). Hours are
cumulative (meaning M1+M2+M3+M4= Total service-learning hours at Carle Illinois). For
MD/PhD students, service-learning activities that take place during the PhD years are not
counted in the Carle Illinois service-learning program.
Background: Service-Learning (SL) provides educational opportunities outside the classroom,
including understanding the needs of the community and responding to those needs through
service-based opportunity. SL can be required through a course/clinical or extracurricular.
Student may engage in both required and extracurricular SL.
I. Course/Clinical SL: This type of SL is required through a course or clinical and are not
counted towards total number of hours for SL. These include:
a. Family Medicine Clinical
b. Service-Learning Elective
II. Extracurricular SL: This type of SL can take two forms, student-initiated and Carle Illinois
initiated.
c. Student-initiated: Students create their own experiences. It can take a variety of
different forms, such as educational programming, mentorship, organizing a
community event, etc. Students are encouraged to pick a topic they are passionate
about that meets a community need.
d. Carle Illinois initiated: Faculty and staff create experiences for students to attend.
These can take a variety of forms, such as community events, research projects, etc.
Policy:
I. To receive special recognition for SL activities, a student must:
a. Complete 100 hours to receive a cord to wear at convocation.
b. Complete 75 hours to add a special designation under the “awards” section of the
residency application (e.g. ERAS).
e. For fairness, SL hours will be prorated to accommodate the timing of this policy.
1. Class of 2025: 25 hours for cord, 20 hours for SL award on residency
application
2. Class of 2026: 40 hours for cord, 30 hours for SL award on residency
application
3. Class of 2027: 65 hours for cord, 45 hours for SL award on residency
application
4. Class of 2028 and beyond: will be subject to the full policy
II. For special recognition or award purposes, SL hours must come from at least five events.
This allows students to diversify how they serve their community.
III. Opportunities will be sent out via email by the Coordinator of Community Partnerships
or their designee. Student project leads may also communicate opportunities to their
peers, but the event must be pre-registered and approved by the SL office.
IV. Community Service VS Service Learning
a. Community service: includes activities to improve the quality of life for community
residents or to solve problems related to the community. These events are not
connected to what students are learning in the classroom, and a reflection is not
required. While students can record their time offering these services, the service is
not in a clinical/educational context.
Example: sorting food at local food back, donating blood (if you are medically able;
1 donation = 1 hour), cleaning up a park, etc.
b. Service-learning: It is a combination of community service, student preparation
(including the ability to identify and define the community need) and write a
reflection. Service-learning is connected to what students are learning in the
classroom.
Examples:
1. SNMA Doctor for a Day – this program focuses on getting young people
interested in medicine, specifically those historically excluded from medicine.
2. Health fairs – Going to community members to educate them on specific
health needs in the community.
c. If students are unsure if their idea is community service or service-learning they can
speak with the Coordinator of Community Partnerships (or their designee) for a
determination.
V. Guidelines
a. Service-learning and community service credit is hour-for-hour unless specified in
writing.
b. A reflection must be completed for hours to count towards service-learning.
c. A community service form must be completed to count towards hours.
d. Reflection forms must be submitted on the SL intranet page within 30 days of the
completion of the event.
e. Prior SL or community service work can be reported to the SL Office for record
keeping, however, those hours will not count towards special recognition/regalia
cords.
