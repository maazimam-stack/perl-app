Purpose: Carle Illinois College of Medicine seeks to promote a culture of integrative health
and wellness among its students, faculty, and staff. Additionally, CI MED will develop a
proactive approach to address topics related to wellness including but not limited to work-
life balance, physician burnout, stress management, belonging.
Policy: There are a wide range of wellness resources available to the students enrolled in
the Carle Illinois College of Medicine. These resources are available directly through CI
MED, the larger UIUC campus, and Champaign-Urbana community.
CI MED will employ a licensed level mental health clinician to oversee the wellness
operations housed within Student Affairs at CI MED. This will include the following services:
Emergency Triage
During regular business hours students may access the following emergency triage
services on a walk-in basis, through contacting the Student Wellness Director directly or
Carle Illinois Student Wellness wellness@medicine.illinois.edu : risk assessments, safety
planning, de-escalation, and facilitating transportation to inpatient hospitalization and
higher levels of care. If a licensed or licensed supervised provider is not available during
regular business hours, students will be directed to the other campus resources including
McKinley Health Center, Counseling Center, UIPD REACH team. During after hours,
weekends, and scheduled breaks, students will be directed to the following community
resources for mental health emergencies: Counseling and Wellness Resources – Carle
Illinois College of Medicine.
Clinical Case Management
CI MED eligible students can contact wellness@medicine.illinois.edu to schedule the
following case management services: information about wellness resources, support
navigating the system of care, referrals to local resources.
Mental Health Therapy
The Director of Student Wellness will provide and oversee evidence based mental health
therapy services to CI MED eligible students to address a variety of mental health
concerns. Therapy services follow a flexible care model to balance the needs of the
students with the limitations of availability, scope of practice, students’ access to
alternative sources of therapy, and potential conflicts of interest. Therapy through CI MED
will include comprehensive psychosocial assessment, treatment planning, and
predominantly be short-term basis (8-12 sessions). Frequency of sessions will be
determined by student need, risk factors, availability, and follow best practice standards of
care. On occasions when a student’s access to other therapy resources is limited, or a
referral to community provider would present risk to the student, longer-term therapy may
be available. When CI MED students require a higher level of care (inpatient, intensive
outpatient, etc.) or specialized care that is outside the scope of practice of CI MED mental
health provider(s), they will receive referrals to recommended treatment and support with
accessing appropriate treatment resources.
Eligibility: Students who are registered as MD students at CI MED are eligible for mental
health therapy services within Student Affairs. This includes students who are on an
approved leave of absence for any approved reason as indicated in APS-9. Immediate
family members and partners who are not enrolled as students are not eligible for
counseling services. This does not include students exclusively enrolled in the “post bac”
or PhD programs. Students who are not located within the state of Illinois, must only
receive clinical services from a therapist who is a member of PSYPACT to remain compliant
with clinical licensing regulations.
Confidentiality: CI MED therapy services will adhere to privacy and confidentiality laws
that govern best practices for mental health services. This includes the Illinois
Confidentiality Act, Mental Health and Developmental Disabilities Act, Family Educational
Rights and Privacy Act, HIPAA Privacy Rule, and Security Rule. Accordingly, communication
between a therapist and client is protected by law. We can only release information about
treatment directly to a student and others with a signed Authorization form that meets legal
requirements. According to FAQs about Reporting – At Illinois We Care , when information
about sexual misconduct is shared with a mental health therapist this does not warrant
mandated reporting to the police or university Title IX office. However, there are some
situations where therapists are permitted or required to disclose information without
student consent or authorization. The following list includes some circumstances where a
student is not required to authorize disclosure:
• Occasionally it may be beneficial for the therapist to consult with other licensed
mental health providers on the treatment. In these circumstances, every effort will
be made to avoid revealing identity. Other professionals are also legally bound to
keep the information confidential. All consultations will be noted in the clinical
record.
• When students present with risk of harm to self or others, we may be obligated to
seek hospital treatment, contact family members, or others who can help provide
protection.
• In response to a valid and enforceable subpoena or other applicable court order,
subject to the limitations of therapist-client privilege.
• If a government agency is requesting information for health oversight activities or
homeland security pursuant to their legal authority, we may be required to provide it
for them.
• If a student files a complaint or lawsuit against their therapist, CI MED may disclose
relevant information regarding treatment to defend themselves before a court or
licensing board.
• Peer reviews for quality control with campus clinicians who adhere to the same
privacy guidelines and only have limited access to content to ensure privacy and
best practices. CI MED Peer Review SOP.
• Periodic mental health audit to ensure quality of care and best practices are being
adhered to.
• If a student files a worker’s compensation claim, we must, upon appropriate
request, disclose information relevant to the claimant’s condition, to the worker’s
compensation claim.
• If the therapist has knowledge of a child, under 18 y/o, or they reasonably suspect
that a child has been the victim of child abuse or neglect (including physical abuse,
sexual abuse, and medical neglect), the law requires that a report of suspected
child abuse is filed with the appropriate governmental agency (IL Department of
Child & Family Services). The therapist may make a report if they know or
reasonably suspect that mental suffering has been inflicted upon a child or that a
child’s emotional wellbeing is endangered in any other way. Once such a report is
filed, your therapist may be required to provide additional information to the
authorities.
• If the therapist observes or obtains knowledge of an incident(s) that reasonably
appears to be physical abuse, isolation, abandonment, abduction, neglect, and/or
financial of an elder (person 65 yrs or older), or a dependent adult, the law requires
therapists to report to the appropriate government agency. Once such a report is
filed, they may also be required to provide additional information.
• If a student expresses a serious threat of physical violence against an identifiable
victim, the therapist must take protective actions, including the potential victim,
and contact the police. The therapist may also seek hospitalization for student’s
safety or contact others who can assist in providing protection.
• If the therapist has reasonable cause to believe that a student is in such mental or
emotional condition as to be gravely disabled, they may be obligated to take
protective action, including seeking hospitalization or contacting family members,
or others who can help provide protection.
• If a student presents with imminent danger to themselves or others, the therapist
must notify the Il Firearm Owner’s Identification Database.
• Other instances required or allowable per law
Conflict of Interest
CI MED Director of Student Wellness and any other positions within the CI MED wellness
program providing clinical mental health services will not have any impact on student
progress or promotion in any capacity. Including but not limited to: providing absence
documentation related to mental health conditions, providing feedback to CSPP, or have
students mandated by CSPP to receive services.
When a conflict of interest related to dual relationships between a student and CI MED
clinician that impacts clinical care, the Director of Wellness or acting clinician will refer the
student to campus or community providers who can meet the student’s needs. When
possible, the decision will be made collaboratively with the student and based on best
clinical judgement, and decision making will be documented in the clinical record.
When referrals are made for any reason to mental health providers outside of CI MED,
clinician will follow ethical guidelines to provide a minimum of 3 referral sources of
providers who are qualified to treat students’ presenting problems. Referral sources will be
documented in students’ clinical record.
If a mental health therapist is employed by CI MED and sees clients through a private or
different practice, and needs to refer a student out due to change in eligibility for services
through CI MED, the therapist may only refer to themselves if this supports the students’
clinical needs, the therapist provides a minimum of 3 additional referral sources, and
documents the clinical implications of this decision.
Documentation/treatment records
Treatment records will be securely stored in a health record, while maintaining strict
adherence to the appropriate privacy laws. Mental health records will be retained and
securely stored for up to 10 years following treatment termination. Records may be
requested by properly completing, signing and submitting Authorization to Disclose
document. All treatment notes will be reviewed and signed by a licensed clinical mental
health provider. Student clinical records are property of University of Illinois Urbana
Champaign.
CI MED Director of Student Wellness will participate in a peer review process of clinical
records with the College of Veterinary Medicine Director of Counseling & Wellbeing.
Consideration for engagement of other campus partners providing mental health treatment
can be made. As indicated in the “Peer Review SOP”, this procedure is in line with the
campus mental health audit recommendations and procedures among other campus units
providing mental health treatment.
Processes/Procedures/Guidelines:
Students can be referred to therapy services w/in CI MED by an academic advisor, faculty
member, campus partner, or self-refer.
Scheduling will be available online and directly with the Director of Student Wellness via an
email request and the secure electronic health record system. Telehealth will be available
to students who are within the state of Illinois to remain in compliance with the licensing
requirements held by the treatment provider, and at the discretion of the Director of
Wellness. Students who are out of state can access ongoing mental health care with a
licensed provider who is registered with the Psychology Interjurisdictional Compact
(PSYPACT)
Cancellation:
Students are asked to provide 24-hours advance notice of cancellation. If students must
cancel, they’re encouraged to provide as much notice as possible so the treatment time
can be offered to another student in need. If possible, another time will be made available
to reschedule during the same week. If multiple appointments are missed without notice
during the semester, the Director of Student Wellness reserves the right to terminate
treatment while providing referral information as alternative treatment options.
Therapy Fees:
There are no fees for mental health services through the Carle Illinois College of Medicine
Student Affairs department. If referrals are made to community providers for specialized
treatment, evaluations, or more intensive care, students will be responsible for the amount
contracted with their health insurance company for out-of-pocket costs.
CI MED Informed Consent
Students will be provided with a copy of the informed consent to treatment prior to or upon
their first appointment for clinical services. They will sign the document prior to initiation
of treatment.
