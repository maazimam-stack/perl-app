Definitions
Accepted Applicant or Acceptee
A person who has applied to the Carle Illinois College of Medicine (Carle Illinois) Doctor of
Medicine program and has been offered conditional admission.
Matriculant
Any accepted Doctor of Medicine program applicant who has attended the Carle Illinois College of
Medicine Doctor of Medicine orientation, for their most recent matriculation year, immediately
preceding enrollment in the first credit-bearing course of the curriculum.
Student
A person currently enrolled and attending Carle Illinois College of Medicine Doctor of Medicine
courses and for whom the University of Illinois maintains an academic record.
5-Panel Urine Drug Testing
A 5-panel drug test or screen that targets the presence of amphetamines, cocaine, opiates,
phencyclidine (PCP), and methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDMA).
Carle Illinois designated Medical Review Officer (MRO)
Medical Review Officer ensures security, data protection, compliance, and procedural handling
of positive screens.
Policy
Accepted Applicants to the Carle Illinois College of Medicine Doctor of Medicine program are
required to undergo a 5-panel drug screening prior to matriculating at CI Med.
Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to ensure that all Doctor of Medicine students entrusted with the
health, safety, and welfare of patients operate in clinical settings with unimpaired judgment.
Substance abuse can impair a student’s capacity to function safely in such a setting, and
compromise patient safety. Students begin interacting with patients within the first four weeks of
school; CI Med must receive drug screening results one week prior to orientation week.
Cause for Drug Screening
The Associate Dean for Student Affairs may require a Doctor of Medicine student to submit to a
drug test at any time “for cause,” based upon:
• Reasonable suspicion of substance abuse.
• Unauthorized use or possession of illegal drugs, alcohol, cannabis on campus or in a
health care setting; or
• Use or possession of illicit drugs at any time.
Reasonable suspicion of substance abuse may be based upon, but is not limited to, the following
criteria:
• Direct observation of the student’s behavior consistent with the use of illegal drugs,
alcohol, or cannabis or a change in behavior such as absenteeism, tardiness, or
significant deterioration of performance.
• A report of illegal drug, alcohol, or cannabis use provided by a credible source that has
been confirmed by the Associate Dean of Student Affairs;
• The student is believed to pose a risk to self or others.
• The student is involved in an accident or incident that results in potential harm or
actual harm to another person.
The drug screen results, and related information are confidential medical information. Upon
receipt of the accepted applicant’s signed consent form the results may be shared with clinical
training sites affiliated with Carle Illinois to avoid the need for duplicate drug tests and assure
training sites that the student has complied with their drug testing policies.
Although Illinois law allows for the use of medical cannabis and recreational cannabis in certain
circumstances, federal laws prohibit cannabis use, possession, and distribution on property
owned by institutions of higher education or in any activities operated by such institutions. In
addition, Illinois law states that colleges and universities are not prevented from prohibiting
cannabis, consistent with federal law. As such, the use, possession, and distribution of cannabis at
the Carle Illinois, elsewhere on the University of Illinois campuses (or other University property),
or in connection with any part of the University’s educational or other activities, regardless of
where those activities occur, remains prohibited. Moreover, to rotate at clinical sites, all Carle
Illinois Doctor of Medicine students are bound by the policies and procedures of the clinical site,
which may be more restrictive and forbid the use of certain substances even if that use is off-site
or would otherwise be considered legal under state or local law (e.g., medical cannabis, cannabis
for recreational use).
Procedure
Accepted Applicants will be responsible for securing a medical office/vendor to conduct their
drug screening and are also responsible for payment of this service. The results will need to be
uploaded and reported through the designated system. The designated MRO will be
responsible for reviewing the results and verifying compliance. Accepted applicants will be
asked to sign a consent form allowing the drug test and the release of test results to those
with a need to know under this policy.
Testing Prior to Matriculation: Carle Illinois will provide each acceptee with information about
the process to complete the required 5- panel drug screen in the onboarding information.
An accepted applicant who refuses drug testing will be referred to the Associate Dean for
Student Affairs. The Associate Dean of Student Affairs will review each case, and it may result
in their acceptance to the Carle Illinois College of Medicine Doctor of Medicine program being
rescinded.
Positive Drug Screen: If the result of the drug screen is reported to the MRO as NON-negative or
positive, the MRO will call the accepted applicant and inquire about their use of medications
prescribed by a physician. The MRO will inform the accepted applicant on how they can verify
that they are on physician prescribed medications. If the MRO approves a medical explanation
for use, the result will be changed to negative.
A NON-negative drug screen result, with no medical explanation for use approved by the MRO,
could result in a decision to rescind the accepted applicant’s offer of admission. The Associate
Dean of Student Affairs reviews each case involving a positive or NON-negative test result.
Decisions to rescind an offer of admission are made on a case-by-case basis that considers an
individualized assessment of the circumstances. The accepted applicant may appeal to the
Associate Dean for Student Affairs in writing prior to the first day of class. As part of the appeal,
the accepted applicant will be able to explain the NON-negative result.
Drug Screen Result other than Positive or Negative: If the drug screen result is neither positive
nor negative, then the test must be repeated (at the accepted applicant’s expense). A test
result that is neither positive nor negative includes but is not limited to a Negative Dilute,
Positive Dilute, Positive No Contact, or Cancelled specimen.
Testing After Matriculation: Students who are required to undergo drug testing for cause after
matriculation must present to a mutually acceptable, third-party drug screen vendor that is
independent of the University and the Carle Illinois College of Medicine’s clinical training
affiliates.
Students who test positive may be subject to disciplinary sanctions and professional
standards. A violation of Carle Illinois professional standards and/or the University of Illinois
student code will initiate the due process for the student.
Students who refuse drug testing will be cited with unprofessional behavior and refusal will
delay starting or suspend participation in the Carle Illinois program and related activity. If
students disagree with the decision, they may follow the Procedures for Appeals,
Grievances, Petitions, outlined in the student handbook.
