Exposure Procedures for Carle Illinois Clinical and Research Experiences at Carle Health and
Affiliates.
Methods of Prevention
Students need to be aware of preventive measures for avoiding infectious diseases transmitted
by bloodborne pathogens such as Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), Hepatitis B Virus
(HBV), Hepatitis C Virus (HCV), as well as body fluid exposure, such as COVID-19, during their
undergraduate medical education. Accordingly, all students should be familiar with universal
and standard precautions, blood and body fluid exposure procedures, and treatment available
at the teaching hospitals, clinics or facilities in which clinical teaching/learning activities occur,
as well as services available through the University of Illinois McKinley Health Center and/or
their personal health insurance.
All students must complete required training regarding safety and environmental exposures
prior to clinical experiences. Office of Students Affairs ensures students are compliant with
these training requirements. If you need additional information, please contact the office of
Student Affairs at studentaffairs@medicine.illinois.edu.
For campus protocols for Bloodborne Pathogen Exposure Control Plan, visit
https://drs.illinois.edu/site-documents/IllinoisExposureControlPlan.pdf.
For Carle Hospital protocols for Bloodborne Pathogen Exposure Control Plan, visit
https://carle.org/services/occupational-medicine Care and Treatment after an Exposure
Immediate actions:
1. Seek first aid and initial care at the clinical location where the injury occurred and
consult immediately with the supervising clinician or their designee overseeing your
clinical experience for medical care.
2. Record a detailed description of the exposure (type of injury or exposure, instrument
involved, type of fluid, location of injury and setting).
3. Notify key clinical personnel at the site including supervising clinician.
Actions within two hours:
1. Evaluation of risk by designated clinician which is usually occupational medicine or a
trained emergency room physician for laboratory testing, treatment, referral, post
exposure prophylaxis or other appropriate actions for the safety and protection of the
student, and others who may be at risk.
2. Discuss with designated clinician (i.e., occupational medicine or trained emergency
room physician) the recommendations for follow up and possible long-term treatment,
testing, and/or referral(s).
Actions within 24 hours:
1. Notify the Office of Student Affairs of the incident and include the detailed description
of the exposure. Email Associate Dean of Student Affairs at
studentaffairs@medicine.illinois.edu or call 217-244-9061.
2. A referral to the nearest hospital emergency department, local health clinic or to the
student's physician for further diagnosis, treatment, and follow-up if needed.
You may also Dial-a-nurse (217-333-2700 press option #2) through McKinley Heath Center 24
hours per day, 7 days per week, and the nurse can advise you on availability of services at
McKinley.
Actions after post exposure care:
1. Any student who needs post-care treatment because of injuries and or medical conditions
from pathogen exposure in a clinical setting may be eligible to apply for reasonable
accommodations following the procedures outlined at:
https://www.disability.illinois.edu/applying-services.
2. Students whose learning activity is interrupted as a result of an exposure incident,
including being isolated or infected by COVID, should contact their Clerkship Director,
clerkship coordinator, Director of Clinical Education and the Associate Dean for Student
Affairs. The Associate Dean for Student Affairs will work with Academic Affairs and other
appropriate units to facilitate the student’s absence and develop an alternative
educational or clinical activity in order to meet educational objectives.
Responsibilities of faculty at clinical sites
1. Provisions for the care of students who sustain needle-stick injuries, eye splashes, cuts,
burns or other exposures are to be supervised by faculty at clinical sites.
2. Carle Illinois Office of Academic Affairs is responsible for informing clinical faculty and
residents about Carle Illinois’ post exposure policy.
Medical evaluation and management costs post exposure:
1. Students need to check with their personal health insurance and clinical sites policies for
coverage of evaluation and, if needed, long-term care.
2. The ongoing treatment is billed to the student’s insurance and may lead to out of pocket
of expenses.
3. Carle Illinois will reimburse up to $2000 of out-of-pocket medical expenses for counseling
and post-exposure treatment for incidents within the first three days (72 hours) of
exposure. Reimbursement claims must be submitted within 90 days to the Office of
Student Affairs. Any additional expenses beyond $2000 will be the responsibility of the
student.
4. After 72 hours, students will be responsible for any other costs related to the incident.
5. Student’s health insurance is expected to cover any additional health care costs.
6. Students will notify the Office of Student Affairs of financial issues pertaining to the
pathogen exposure by emailing the Associate Dean of Student Affairs at
studentaffairs@medicine.illinois.edu
7. If Carle Illinois students have unanswered questions or need additional support, they can
contact the Office of Student Affairs at 217-300-9391 or email
studentaffairs@medicine.illinois.edu.
