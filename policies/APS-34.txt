Carle Illinois College of Medicine (Carle Illinois) is committed to fostering an inclusive and
accessible environment for all students, who participate in Carle Illinois programs or services.
Carle Illinois will ensure that students, have access to university facilities, technology, and
information needed to have an equal opportunity for access in their education.
Carle Illinois is committed to complying with state and federal laws regarding individuals with
disabilities, including, but not limited to: Sections 504 of the 1973 Rehabilitation Act (Section
504), the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA), the ADA Amendments Act of 2008
(ADAAA).
Carle Illinois does not discriminate against individuals with disabilities. Qualified individuals
shall not be excluded from admission or participation in Carle Illinois’ educational programs and
activities solely by reason of their disability or medical condition.
The MD degree is a broad, undifferentiated degree attesting to general knowledge in medicine
and the basic skills required for the practice of medicine. Essential abilities and characteristics
required for completion of the MD degree are needed to assure that candidates for admission,
promotion and graduation are able to complete the entire course of study and participate fully
in all aspects of medical training with reasonable accommodations, if required.
Carle Illinois intends for its Doctor of Medicine program graduates to become competent and
compassionate physicians who are capable of pursuing and completing graduate medical
education, passing licensing exams, and obtaining and maintaining medical licensure. All
medical students, regardless of specialization or aspirations for a non-clinical career, are still
required to achieve competence in the full curriculum required for all students.
Carle Illinois has an ethical responsibility for the safety of patients with whom MD students and
graduates will come in contact. Although students learn and work under the supervision of the
faculty, students interact with patients throughout their medical school education. Patient
safety and well-being are therefore major factors in establishing requirements involving the
program and academic standards of candidates for admission, promotion, and graduation. The
essential abilities and characteristics described herein are also referred to as technical (or non-
academic) standards. They are described in the Carle Illinois Student handbook.
Carle Illinois provides reasonable accommodation in its academic programs to individuals with
disabilities who have approved accommodations with the Disability Resources and Educational
Services (DRES). A reasonable accommodation is a modification or adjustment that provides an
equal opportunity while maintaining the program and academic standards set by Carle Illinois.
Included in Carle Illinois syllabi are the suggested text provided by the Provost office for
students with disabilities.
Students enrolled in the MD program initiate the process of applying for services with the It is
the student’s responsibility to initiate all accommodation requests and Carle Illinois expects
students must submit accommodation requests to DRES as soon as possible. Carle Illinois
requires letters of accommodation from DRES at least four weeks in advance of the curricular
event. This lead time provides appropriate notification to coordinate the logistics for
implementing accommodations with preceptors, clinical sites, course/clerkship directors, and
appropriate staff. Failure to submit documentation on time may result in delay in providing or
difficulty implementing the accommodations. Eligibility for disability accommodations will be
determined by DRES after a student completes the application process.
Receipt and Coordination of Accommodations
The Associate Dean for Student Affairs (ADSA) or their liaison serves as the designated
accommodation contact for Carle Illinois medical students. Students who want
accommodations must deliver the official DRES letter of accommodation to the ADSA. This
position’s role is to:
• Facilitate the coordination of the interactive process for Carle Illinois with DRES and the
MD student;
• Identify and contact the individuals within Carle Illinois who are necessary for
implementing the accommodation, including those who have oversight and decision-
making authority for implementing the accommodations and facilitate the notification
process to these individuals;
• Inform the student requesting accommodations of the Carle Illinois policies and
procedures for implementing accommodations; and the DRES policies and procedures
for implementation, which are outlined in the letter of academic accommodation,
including notifying the appropriate course directors
• Support the student throughout the interactive process and implementation of the
accommodations; and
• Facilitate questions with compliance of accommodations with DRES Access Specialist as
appropriate.
Letters of accommodation are confidential and only shared within Carle Illinois on a need-to-
know basis.
Students may appeal a decision of accommodations from DRES by contacting the Office of
Access and Equity.
For United States Medical Licensing Exams, Step 1 and Step 2, students must request
accommodations directly to the National Board of Medical Examiners.
For employment accommodations, students may request accommodations directly through the
Office of Access and Equity. https://oae.illinois.edu/.
Students who experience sex-based harassment or discrimination can report their experience
to the Office of Title IX. The Title IX Coordinator (titleixcoordinator@illinois.edu) may provide
“Supportive Measures” to help the student preserve access to educational programs and
activities. This process can be independent of the ADA accommodations process.
