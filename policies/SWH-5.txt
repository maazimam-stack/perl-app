Policy Purpose
Carle Illinois College of Medicine (Carle Illinois) requires proof of immunity for Doctor of
Medicine students to certain vaccine-preventable diseases, to protect patients, students,
faculty, staff, and the public. This is done in accordance with Illinois state law, University of
Illinois regulations, and requirements from clinical partners. Additional items may be required
by affiliated clinical facilities such as, a 5-panel drug screen, Basic Life Support (BLS)
certification, and a mask fit test.
Definitions
Accepted Applicant or Acceptee
A person who has applied to the Carle Illinois College of Medicine Doctor of Medicine program
and has been offered conditional admission.
Matriculant
Any accepted Doctor of Medicine program applicant who has attended Carle Illinois Doctor of
Medicine orientation, for their most recent matriculation year, immediately preceding
enrollment in the first credit-bearing course of the curriculum.
Student
A person currently enrolled and attending Carle Illinois Doctor of Medicine courses and for
whom the University of Illinois maintains an academic record.
Booster
A dose of an immunizing substance given to maintain or renew the effect of a previous
immunization.
Titer
A laboratory test that measures the presence and number of antibodies in the blood.
Immunization Requirements
The following immunizations are required to matriculate and to progress through the Carle
Illinois curriculum. Some immunizations must be completed annually to remain in compliance
as noted below. Documentation of dates of disease is not acceptable evidence of immunity.
Additional or updated vaccination requirements may be added at any time and students will be
given a minimum of 30 days to allow for vaccination administration so they may meet the
updated requirements.
COVID-19
You must be up to date with current CDC guidelines.
Hepatitis B
1. Documentation of a series of 2 or 3 doses depending on the brand of the vaccine.
2. A titer must be drawn 1-2 months after the last dosage.
3. If the titer is negative (<10 mIU/mL), administer an additional dose of Hep B vaccine
followed by titer testing 1-2 months later.
4. If the titer is positive (>10 mIU/mL), the student is immune.
5. If the titer remains negative, complete the second series of vaccine followed by an
additional titer 1-2 months after the last dose.
6. If a student has completed 2 full vaccination series without producing adequate protective
antibodies, they are considered a non-responder. Non-responders to Hepatitis B will follow
applicable guidelines from the CDC.
Influenza
Documentation of a flu vaccine administered annually during flu season.
Measles-Mumps-Rubella (MMR)
1. Documentation of 2 vaccination doses at least 28 days apart, after 12 months of age, and
both doses given after 12/31/1967.
2. A positive serum titer within the past 10 years is also acceptable proof of immunity against
measles, mumps, and rubella.
Meningococcal Conjugate Vaccine
1. Students between the ages of 16-21 must have one dose of Menactra, Menveo, Nimenrix or
Aramen on or after their 16th birthday. Meningococcal-B vaccine does not meet this
requirement.
2. Students aged 22 and over are not required to receive the vaccine.
Tetanus-Diphtheria-Pertussis (DPT, DTP, DT, DTaP, Td, Tdap)
At least 3 doses diphtheria, tetanus and pertussis containing vaccine are required. One dose
must be Tdap. The last dose of vaccine (DPT, DTP, DT, DTaP, Td, Tdap) or booster must have
been administered within 10 years of the student’s matriculation date. Td or Tdap boosters are
required every 10 years from the date of the last administered booster dose.
Tuberculosis (TB) Testing
1. Initial proof of no active TB infection via one of the following methods:
a. Negative 2-step TB skin test (1-3 weeks apart)
b. Negative QuantiFERON-TB Gold blood test
c. A clear chest x-ray if either of above is positive. Follow up with symptom review from
thereafter on a yearly basis.
2. Annual proof of no active TB infection via one of the following methods:
a. Negative 1-step TB skin test
b. Negative QuantiFERON-TB Gold blood test
c. Annual submission of the symptom review form if initial 2-step TB skin test or
QuantiFERON-TB Gold blood test results were positive.
Varicella (Chickenpox):
1. Documentation of 2 vaccinations, at least 28 days apart, OR
2. A lab report showing positive titers.
a. If a titer is negative, follow up with two boosters four (4) to eight (8) weeks apart.
Students are not required to seek additional titer to confirm/disconfirm immunity after
receiving the two boosters.
Procedures
Accepted Doctor of Medicine applicants and students are responsible for uploading
required documentation by designated deadlines into the system specified by Carle Illinois.
Students are typically emailed reminders sixty (60), thirty (30), and fifteen (15) days prior to
item expiration dates. Documents are monitored and approved by a vendor and a designated
Carle Illinois Student Affairs staff member. Costs associated with immunizations or lab tests are
the responsibility of the accepted applicant or student.
Exemptions
Leave of Absence
Students who are on leave for the Doctor of Medicine program are exempt from immunization
requirements but are required to be in full compliance before they will be permitted to return
to Carle Illinois.
Medical Exemption
A written, signed, and dated statement from a physician stating the specific contraindicated
vaccine(s), the reason and duration of the medical exemption is required. Documents are
reviewed and approved by the Medical Director at McKinley.
Pregnancy or Suspected Pregnancy
A signed and dated statement from a physician indicating that you are pregnant (include
expected date of delivery) is required. After delivery, you must then comply with immunization
requirements as directed by your physician.
Religious Exemption
Submit the Petition for Exemption to the Dean of Students at helpdean@illinois.edu.
Immunization Non-Compliance
Accepted Doctor of Medicine applicant immunization records are due at least seven (7) days
prior to matriculation. If an accepted applicant does not fulfill the immunization requirements
by that deadline, their offer of acceptance to the Carle Illinois Doctor of Medicine program may
be rescinded.
If a Doctor of Medicine student does not fulfill the annual immunization requirements and
other items required by affiliated clinical facilities by stated deadlines, they may be prohibited
from beginning or continuing their education and clinical experiences.
