Policy Number: APS-16
1. Purpose:
Carle Illinois College of Medicine (CIMED) supports ongoing student learning by administering
frequent assessments that help monitor progress and the acquisition of required knowledge and
skills, while establishing consistent grading guidelines that apply to all CIMED MD program courses,
required and non-required.
2. Definitions:
Course: Any component of the MD program curriculum assigned to a university subject code and
course number in which a student receives, or will receive, a final summative grade.
Assignment: A task or activity designed to promote learning and evaluate student understanding.
Assessment: A process or strategy used to gather information and make judgments about an
individual’s knowledge, skills, or performance.
Formative Assessment (no- to low-stakes): Assessment for learning, intended to provide ongoing
feedback that helps MD students identify strengths and areas for improvement, and assists faculty
in addressing learning needs during the course.
Summative Assessment (high stakes): Assessment of learning, used to make decisions about MD
student achievement at the end of a course or instructional unit by comparing performance against
defined standards or benchmarks.
Criterion-Based Grading: Evaluation of an MD student’s performance according to a set of clearly
defined standards or criteria.
Normative-Based Grading: Evaluation of an MD student’s performance based on comparison with
the performance of peers.
Make-up: An opportunity to complete missed assessments or assignments. In cases of illness or
emergency, MD students must notify the course or clerkship director as soon as reasonably possible
and provide appropriate documentation to qualify for a makeup opportunity, if feasible.
Related policies: APS-17 Reassessment and Remediation, APS-7 Attendance Absence and Vacation
3. Assessment and Assessment Completion Policy:
3.1 Quizzes
3.1.1: Quizzes are components of non-clinical and clinical courses. Students are expected to
complete formative quizzes within the specified times to get appropriate feedback. Quizzes in
the pre-clerkship years are graded and contribute to the final course grade. Courses may set
quiz weighting and the inclusion of other graded components (e.g., practicals, simulations,
project-based assessments). All assessment weights must be explicitly stated in the course
syllabus and available to students prior to the start of the course.
To promote learning, quizzes will include three attempts, with the highest score recorded. Some
courses may use alternative assessment formats and may not use quizzes; in such cases,
assessment weight will be allocated to other graded components as described in the syllabus.
3.2 Team-based Learning (TBL)
3.2.1: TBL activities include an Individual Assessment Readiness Test (IRAT), a Team Readiness
Assessment Test (TRAT) and the Application Exercise. Students are expected to complete the
IRAT and TRAT. The composite score for a student is 60% IRAT, 40% TRAT, while application
exercises are non-graded, attendance and active participation are required to provide feedback
on this meaningful part of TBL.
3.2.2: Students who miss a TBL due to an approved absence will work with the Course Director
and the DEA for makeup.
3.2.3: Students who miss a TBL for reasons other than an approved absence will receive a zero
for the TBL.
3.3 Midterm and Final Examinations
3.3.1: Foundational science courses have a required final exam at the end of each course.
Courses longer than six weeks also have midterm exams. Midterm and final exams are offered
either through the NBME Customized Assessment Services (CAS) system or through another
assessment delivery platform and will be administered under NBME proctoring guidelines.
Feedback to students is provided after the midterm and after the final in the form of an
individualized performance report.
3.3.2: All mid-term and final examinations carry assigned weight and contribute to the final
grade per the course syllabus.
3.3.3: Students who miss a final or midterm exam due to an approved absence will be able to
make-up the examination later in consultation with a Course Director and the DEA.
3.3.4: Students who miss a final or midterm exam without an approved absence will receive zero
points for that missed attempt. Re-assessment of the exam will follow according to the course
syllabus and APS-17.
3.4 Progress Tests
3.4.1: Students are required to complete three scheduled formative Progress Tests as part of
BSE 666 Academic Progress I. CI MED Progress Tests are the Comprehensive Basic Science
Examination (CBSE), a comprehensive subject exam, administered by the NBME and adhere to
their proctoring policies. The Progress Tests are not graded. Students receive feedback in the
form of an individualized performance report.
3.4.2: Students who miss a Progress Test due to an approved absence will be able to take the
examination later in consultation with the DEA. Students who miss a Progress Test without an
approved absence will be required to make-up the exam per the course syllabus and may result
in the filing of a professionalism concern.
3.5 Objective Structured Clinical Examinations (OSCEs)
3.5.1: Students must pass all clinical skills tested on a summative OSCE. Students are allowed up
to three attempts at each skill/station. Failure to pass each skill/station within three attempts
will result in a remediation plan as outlined in the corresponding course.
3.5.2: Students who have an approved absence during summative OSCE will be given an
opportunity to take the examination at a later date without penalty in consultation with the
appropriate Course Director and/or Director of Clinical Skills. This will generally occur on a date
when re-assessments are scheduled for other class members.
3.5.3: Students who miss a summative OSCE without an approved absence will be given an
opportunity to take the examination/station at a later date. This will generally occur on a date
when re-assessments are scheduled for other class members. However, the missed session will
count as one of the three allowable attempts.
3.6 Clinical Science Subject Examinations
3.6.1: NBME Clinical Science Subject Examinations (also known as shelf exams) are required and
administered in a proctored setting at the end of each clerkship. All Clinical Subject Examinations
carry an assigned weight and contribute to the final grade per the course syllabus. Students
receive feedback in the form of an individual performance report after the examination.
Satisfactory performance on a shelf exam is deemed to be scoring at or above the fifth
percentile nationally.
3.6.2: The NBME Clinical Mastery Series are self-assessments with feedback designed for
students taking an NBME Clinical Science Subject Exam. These are referred to as the practice
shelf examination. Practice shelf exams are required and administered in a proctored setting at
approximately the midpoint of each clerkship.
3.6.3: Students who miss a shelf examination due to an approved absence (refer to APS-7) will
be able to take the examination later in consultation with the DEA and the Office of Student
Affairs.
3.6.4: Students who miss a shelf examination without an approved absence will receive a failing
grade for that missed attempt. Reassessment of the exam will occur later in consultation with
the DEA.
3.7 Problem-based Learning (PBL)
3.7.1: Basic Science Integrated with Clinical Sciences, Engineering, and Professionalism (BICEP)
cases refer to specific types of Problem-based Learning (PBL) sessions that are used to integrate
basic science with clinical reasoning and patient care decision making.
3.7.2: Attendance and participation are required. BICEP facilitators utilize a scoring rubric to
assess students’ knowledge, skills, and behaviors within these sessions. Students must
acknowledge facilitator feedback in the Evaluation system.
3.7.3: Students who miss a BICEP session due to an absence will work with the facilitator and the
DEA for makeup. Check the course syllabus for the instructions.
3.7.3.1 If the clinical vignette question was generated using AI or obtained from other
resources, students must disclose this information to the facilitator and provide the prompt(s)
used and/or reference to generate the question. This is in addition to other required
information, i.e. generated questions, rationale for correct and incorrect answer choices, and
references.
3.8 Other Assessments
3.8.1: Individual courses may institute assessments that are not listed here, may carry weight
and may contribute to the final grade per the course syllabus. Scoring rubrics must be made
available to students.
3.8.2: Students must complete/submit assignments as outlined in the syllabus and by the
deadlines stated in the syllabus, with appropriate assessment methods. Assignments that are
turned in late will not be accepted, unless otherwise stated by the course syllabus.
3.8.3: Anatomy Table Tests are formative assessments within the Foundational Science courses.
Based on the resources needed, Anatomy Table Tests cannot be made-up regardless of absence.
3.8.4: Anatomy Lab Practicals are summative assessments within the Foundational Science
courses. Students who miss an Anatomy Lab Practical without an approved absence may
complete a re-assessment at a later date, in consultation with the Anatomy Discipline Lead and
the DEA. The maximum score attainable on the re-assessment is 70%.
3.8.5: Simulation-based assessments may be formative or summative conducted in a simulated
clinical environment. Students who miss a scheduled simulation assessment must coordinate a
make-up session with the Simulation team and the DEA.
3.8.6: Research or Project-based assessments or portfolio-based assessments are governed by
rubrics outlined in the course syllabus.
4: Grading Policy
4.1: The criteria for assigning final grades must be clearly posted in the syllabus and made available
to students prior to the start of the course.
4.2: The MD-Curriculum Oversight Committee (MD-COC) has the authority and responsibility to set
standards for achievement for courses and clerkship as well as the entire curriculum. The MD-COC,
in consultation Office of Academic Affairs, is responsible for maintaining grading guidelines and
providing feedback to course directors. All criteria for assigning final grades must be approved by
the MD-COC.
4.3: The clerkships may not change the grading standards while the clinical immersion year is in
progress.
4.4: Students will earn one of five permanent grades for a course based on either normative-based
or criterion-based grading.
4.4.1: Satisfactory (S) – The student’s performance met or exceeded the minimum standards for
passing the course as defined in the grading criteria.
4.4.2: Satisfactory after Remediation (SR) – The student’s performance met or exceeded the
minimum standards for passing the course after required remediation of previously
unsatisfactory performance. See APS-17 for further information about remediation
requirements.
4.4.3: Unsatisfactory (U) – The student’s performance did not meet the minimum standards for
passing the course as defined in the grading criteria.
4.4.4: Honors (HON) – Within the required clerkships, the student’s performance met or
exceeded the standards of achieving honors as defined in the grading criteria. Any student who
meets the established criteria will be eligible to receive Honors.
4.5.1.1: In order to discourage grade inflation and protect the value of an honors grade,
all clerkships will establish a criterion designed so that approximately 20% of students
will earn an honors grade. If this threshold of 20% is not met, scrutiny will be applied to
consider adjustment of the criteria for subsequent years.
4.5.1.2: The honors criteria for the shelf exam can increase across clerkship blocks. The
increase should be guided by the NBME score report for the shelf exam with cutoff
scores listed in the syllabus.
4.4.5: Withdrawal (W) – The student’s performance did not meet the minimum standards for
passing course as defined in the grading criteria because they were unable to complete all
course requirements prior to withdrawing from the course.
4.5 Students may receive temporary grades in certain circumstances.
4.5.1: Incomplete (I) A student who did not complete one or more of the course requirements
may be granted an incomplete with the permission of one of the decanal staff members within
the Office of Academic Affairs. Given the unique nature of the CI MED undergraduate medical
education curriculum, plans for completion of coursework will be drafted in consultation
between the student, the course director(s), and the Offices of Student Affairs and Academic
Affairs. If the incomplete is not resolved by the stated date in the plan, it will be replaced with an
unsatisfactory final grade.
4.5.2: Deferred (DFR): A placeholder until the final grade is available.
4.6: Request for a clerkship grade adjustment must be made in writing to the clerkship Director no
later than 5 weeks after a clerkship’s end date. Students should refer to the Clerkship syllabus for
processes and procedures.
4.6.1: A student may request a review of the following from the Clerkship Director:
4.6.1.1: Faculty/Resident assessment scores
4.6.1.2: Faculty/Resident assessment comments
4.6.1.3: Required assignments (if completed by the established deadline)
4.6.1.4: MSPE summary paragraphs completed by the Clerkship Directors
• Requests for review of MSPE summary paragraphs follow the MSPE review
protocol (see Clerkship syllabus)
4.6.2: A student may NOT request a review of the following from the Clerkship Director:
4.6.2.1: NBME Clinical Subject exam scores
4.6.2.2: Zero or decreased scores for failure to submit required assignments by the
established deadline
4.6.2.3: Failure to complete a required component (i.e., case logs, DOS, Aquifer, etc
